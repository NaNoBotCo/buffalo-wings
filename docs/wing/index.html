Wing CountryWhich part of the bird

A chicken carries two wings. Each one gives a drumette, a flat and a tip — so an order of twenty pieces took ten birds’ worth of one wing apiece, and the tip usually never made it to the table.

The wing, divided

Laid out flat, shoulder end at the left. The two rings mark the joints a knife goes through.

flatthe middle piece, two thin bones, the most skin per bite; Buffalo calls it a wingette, a butcher calls it a flat
tipthe pointed end, skin and cartilage, cut off into the stockpot at most counters

One bird, one of each

What to call them

drumette

Upper of the three wing segments, and the meatiest. One bone, meat bunched at one end, skin all round: it eats like a small drumstick, which is where the name comes from.

flat

Middle segment, two bones with a gap between them, and more skin per bite than any other piece of a chicken. Flat because it is — it lies down in the fryer basket and com

wing tip

Third segment, the pointed end. Skin, cartilage, two slivers of bone, almost no meat.

flapper

Cook's name for the wing tip: the pointed third segment, the one that carried the flight feathers. Skin and cartilage over two thin bones.

party wing

A supermarket's name for wings already broken down: tips off, joint cut, drumettes and flats loose in one bag or tray. The work a bar's prep cook does, done at the plant.
