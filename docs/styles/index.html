City by city: who tosses, who breads, who claims it.

Alabama (1)

Alabama white sauce wings

white barbecue sauce wings, white sauce wings, Decatur-style

Wings smoked over hickory and then coated or dunked in white sauce — a mayonnaise-based barbecue sauce with vinegar, lemon juice, black pepper, salt, and in many versions cayenne and horseradish.

mambo sauce wings, wings and mumbo, carryout wings

Georgia (1)

Lemon pepper wings

lemon pepper dry, lemon pepper wet, LP wings

A wing cooked without breading and finished in lemon pepper — granulated lemon zest mashed with cracked black pepper, then baked and dried, usually with salt and sugar in the blend. Atlanta orders it two ways.

Illinois (1)

Chicago mild sauce wings

mild sauce wings, South Side wings, fried hard with mild

Wings fried hard — left in the oil past golden until the skin goes dark and brittle — then handed over a counter in a box with french fries and a slice of white bread, the whole box doused in mild sauce.

Tennessee (2)

Memphis dry-rub wings

dry wings, dry rub wings, dry heat

A wing finished with the rub that Memphis puts on ribs — salt and a spice blend, house by house, usually paprika-led — and handed over without sauce.

Nashville hot wings

hot chicken wings, hot wings, Nashville style

Hot chicken's method put on a wing: marinate in a water- or buttermilk-based seasoned blend, flour, fry, then paint with a cayenne paste loosened with fat drawn from the fryer.

Other (4)

Garlic parmesan wings

garlic parm, parm wings, garlic parmesan

A fried wing tossed in melted butter or oil with garlic and grated parmesan, often with dried herbs and black pepper, sometimes finished with more cheese out of the bowl so it sits in visible flecks.

Jerk wings

Jamaican jerk wings, jerk chicken wings

Wings dry-rubbed or wet-marinated in Jamaican jerk seasoning and cooked over fire until the outside blackens.
