Salt, by the tablespoon

One dot per bottle, 20 in all; the upright tick marks its row's median. Across every bottle the median runs 227 mg a tablespoon, against the 2,300 mg the FDA counts as a day. Old Bay Seasoning tops it at 1,680 mg. Careful with the far right: 2 of these are dry seasonings whose own serving is a quarter teaspoon or less, scaled to a tablespoon here so they sit on the same axis. Nobody eats a tablespoon of them. What comes first on the label

A label lists ingredients in descending order by weight, so the first word states the sauce's whole argument. Watch for the bracket: aged cayenne peppers are printed with their own vinegar inside the bracket, which is a pepper sauce, not a vinegar one. Numbers on the segments count bottles; the number at the right is the row total.

Where the makers are

One little country per sauce. A dot marks a bottler's town, not a restaurant; several bottles from one town sit on one dot.

Heat nobody measures

1 of 22 bottles carry a Scoville number anybody publishes. Scoville heat units come from a lab assay of capsaicin, and almost no wing-sauce maker pays for one; the word on the front — mild, medium, hot, and whatever a challenge sauce calls itself — is a maker's claim about its own product and nothing more. Each claim sits on its sauce's page as heat_claim, in the maker's own words.

Alabama white sauce

Blue cheese dressing

A thick white dip of blue cheese with mayonnaise and buttermilk, sour cream or yogurt, plus milk, vinegar, onion powder and garlic powder.

Buffalo sauce

Cayenne pepper hot sauce cut with melted butter. Two things, warmed together and held warm, poured over fried wings in a metal bowl and shaken.

Honey barbecue sauce

Hot honey

Honey with chillies in it. Sweet first, then a heat that builds slowly — the flavour writers describe as "sweet-first" with a "slow-building heat".

Jerk marinade

A wet marinade, or a dry rub, built on allspice and Scotch bonnet peppers — both native to Jamaica and grown there by the Taíno.

Lemon pepper

A dry blend of granulated lemon zest and cracked black peppercorns. The zest is mashed into the pepper so citrus oil soaks in, then the mix is baked and dried.

Lemon pepper wet

A wing that has been through something wet and then through lemon pepper.

Mango habanero

A sweet-then-hot wing sauce: mango purée and corn syrup in front, habanero and cayenne behind. Orange, thick, and built to be tossed.

Memphis dry rub

A dry mix of salt, paprika, sugar and spices, patted onto meat before it cooks and shaken over it again after.

Mild sauce

A red-brown condiment poured over fried chicken and wings on Chicago's South and West Sides. Recipes vary a great deal; many versions combine ketchup, barbecue

Mumbo sauce

A carryout condiment of Washington, D.C.: red-orange, thick, poured over fried chicken wings, french fries, fried jumbo shrimp and fried rice.

Nashville hot paste

A cayenne paste, spooned onto fried chicken the moment it leaves the fryer.

Old Bay Seasoning

A blend of eighteen herbs and spices built on celery salt — salt and celery seed — with red pepper, black pepper and paprika.

Ranch dressing

A savoury creamy dressing of buttermilk, salt, garlic, onion, black pepper and herbs — commonly chives, parsley and dill — mixed into mayonnaise or another oil

Soy garlic sauce

A brown glaze of soy sauce, sugar and garlic, brushed onto fried chicken after the fryer. Sweet and savoury rather than hot; often heavily garlicky.

Sweet chili sauce

A clear, thickened red sauce of chillies, rice wine vinegar and sugar, often with garlic and sometimes fish sauce. Red chilli peppers — Fresno, Thai or red jala

Teriyaki sauce

Soy sauce, mirin or sake, and sugar, boiled down to a glaze. The Japanese name for it is tare (タレ).

Yangnyeom sauce

A sweet and spicy glaze built on gochujang — a fermented Korean paste of red chili powder, glutinous rice, fermented soybean powder, barley malt powder and salt
