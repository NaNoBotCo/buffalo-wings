an outfit · MSSouthern Foodways Alliance

also: SFA

An archive that records what cooks say about their own work. The Southern Foodways Alliance is based at the University of Mississippi's Center for the Study of Southern Culture and, in its own words, "documents, studies, and explores the diverse food cultures of the changing American South." It collects oral histories, makes short documentary films, publishes and podcasts as Gravy, and holds an annual symposium. For a wing record, it is the difference between a claim about a kitchen and a transcript from one.

The story Cited · web-sfa-history-founders

Author and activist John Egerton convened a two-day meeting in Birmingham, Alabama on 22 July 1999; fifty people lent their names to the new non-profit. The organising meeting was held at Southern Living, the dinner at Frank and Pardis Stitt's Highlands Bar and Grill. John T. Edge was elected the first director.

What the archive holds matters more here than how it was founded. Amy C. Evans interviewed the Gibson family in Decatur, Alabama about Big Bob Gibson, who was smoking pork and chicken at weekend barbecues in 1925 while working for the L & N Railroad, and whose vinegar-and-mayonnaise white sauce is now on wings across north Alabama. The alliance recorded André Prince Jeffries at Prince's Hot Chicken Shack in Nashville, where the line is longer at ten at night than at lunchtime, and gave her a Guardian of the Tradition award on 11 August 2006.

Inference — an oral history is a source a wing record can cite where a press profile cannot reach: the cook's own sentence, dated, transcribed, and held by a named institution.

How it is done Cited · web-sfa-about

Interviews are recorded, transcribed, and published with the interviewer named and the date given, grouped into projects by place or trade. Films sit beside them. The symposium meets each autumn in Oxford, Mississippi.

Today Cited · web-sfa-about

Still at the Center for the Study of Southern Culture. The 2026 symposium is set for 10–11 October.
