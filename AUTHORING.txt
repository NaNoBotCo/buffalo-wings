WING COUNTRY — WRITING A RECORD
===============================

One JSON file per node, in data/nodes/<type>/<id>.json. The schema is
schema/node.schema.json. Copy an exemplar and change every field:

  data/nodes/style/buffalo.json          a style (rich: what / story / how / today, kin, confusable)
  data/nodes/term/wing.json              a word (etymology block)
  data/nodes/place/anchor-bar.json       a place (geo, address, links, tags, hours)

Run  python3 tools/validate.py  until your records pass. Kin targets nobody has
written yet show as errors; expect that while the set is still being built.

TYPES
  style   a city's way with a wing        sauce   a sauce, a rub or a dip
  dish    a thing eaten beside it         fry     a cut, a coating, an oil, a fryer, a method
  place   a bar, shack, counter, kitchen  person  a cook, founder, family, writer, organizer
  org     a council, trail, archive       event   a festival, a contest, a day
  term    a word, with its root           art     signs, neon, murals, posters
  story   a long read

FIELDS THAT CARRY THE WEIGHT
  names.name     what the tradition calls it. names.said = how a counter says it.
  text.what      one plain paragraph. Present tense. What it is.
  text.story     narrative, told as narrative. Scenes, dates, names. Mark each stretch:
                   plain prose        = cited in this record's sources
                   *Tradition holds —*  general knowledge, hedged
                   *Inference —*        this project's own reasoning
  text.how       how it gets cooked, sauced, served, eaten. text.today = now, dated.
  kin[]          what another node IS to this one, said from here. One sentence a reader
                 standing on this page wants: not "see also" but the fact that joins them
                 ("its celery arrived because Teressa Bellissimo grabbed what the bar had").
                 The other record answers in its own words. Link only to cast-list ids.
  etymology      required on type=term, welcome anywhere a name has a root. root = the
                 chain or the morpheme split; first_attested = a date with a source behind
                 it, else blank; folk etymologies go in note, labelled folk.
  facets         open keys per type, see data/vocab/facets.json: state, cut, base, course,
                 kind, role, status, founded, cooked, heat, styles, when, letter.
  sources[]      ids from data/sources/sources.json ONLY. Add new ones to
                 data/sources/new-<yourname>.json in the same shape; build.py merges them.
                 Wikipedia: s:wp-<slug>. A web page: s:web-<slug> with title, publisher,
                 url and the date you read it. A book: title, author, publisher, year —
                 never an ISBN you did not see.
  provenance     default tier + per-field overrides. Tiers: cited, harvested, tradition,
                 inference, field. A date, a founding year, a death, a lawsuit, a price:
                 cite it or hedge it.
  geo / address  places and events. Coordinates from OSM (data/harvest/osm-places.json) or
                 the address geocoded by hand; precision says which. address.state takes a
                 two-letter code.
  images[]       leave empty. Put candidate Commons titles in x_commons_files:
                 ["File:Buffalo wings.jpg"]; tools/harvest_commons.py fetches them with
                 their licences. Only CC0 / PD / CC BY / CC BY-SA / FAL.
  confidence     high / medium / low. needs_verification: true when a fact stays unchecked.

PLACE FIELDS
  tags[]          {tag, source, tier?, note?, since?, url?}. Keys in data/vocab/tags.json.
                  A tag carries the source it came from. Ownership and welcome tags
                  (black-owned, woman-owned, lgbtq-friendly) rest on the owner's own words,
                  a press profile naming the owner, or a public directory — not on a name,
                  a photograph or a guess. A tag without one of those goes unwritten, and a
                  missing tag says nothing about a place. The validator refuses tier
                  tradition or inference.
  recognitions[]  {by, what, year?, source, url?}. `by` from data/vocab/recognizers.json.
                  One entry per recognizer per place. The count measures attention paid,
                  never our ranking.
  hours           {open[], closed[], text, sold_out, seasonal, source, url, tier, checked}.
                  THREE STATES, NEVER TWO: open, closed, and nobody published it. A day in
                  neither list is unknown and drops out of the filters. Guessing a closure
                  is worse than a blank, because a reader drives on it.
  recipes[]       on sauces and dishes: {title, source, license, year?, url?, author?,
                  book?, page?, ingredients[]?, text[]?, yield?, note?, region?}.
                  Public-domain cookbooks (US, before 1930) copy in full into `text`; CC BY
                  and CC BY-SA texts too, with the source named; everything else is
                  license: "ingredients-only" with ingredients[] and no text. A list of
                  ingredients states a fact. Instructions are somebody's writing.
  profile         on sauces, measured off a label or a published recipe: {kind, base, maker,
                  town, state, ingredients[] in label order, pepper_rank, vinegar_rank,
                  fat_rank, tomato_rank, sugar_rank, mustard_rank, soy_rank (1-based
                  position, 0 absent, null unknown), sodium_mg_per_tbsp, sugar_g_per_tbsp,
                  serving_tbsp, scoville (ONLY where somebody publishes a number),
                  heat_claim (the maker's own word, verbatim), source, url, accessed, note}.
  sections[]      on art and story: [{h, text, images?[indices]}] after text.what.

WORDS AND TONE
  validate.py refuses a banned-word list — read it at the top of tools/validate.py, which
  is where it lives and where it stays current.   <!-- stylecheck: allow --> Wings run on argument —
  Buffalo against Rochester, flat against drum, bone-in against boneless, blue cheese
  against ranch. Report every argument. Join none of them. When somebody says a boneless
  wing is not a wing, quote them and name them; the sentence takes quotation marks or it
  does not go in.
  Chains, frozen bags, air fryers and party packs belong to the tradition and get described
  the same way a bar with a forty-year fryer does.
  Write short. Lead with a verb. Cut "the" and "what" wherever the sentence survives it.
  Fewer adjectives, more verbs. Define every non-obvious word in a clause at first use.
  Prices carry their date. No sentence that announces the next sentence. No closing summary.

VERIFICATION CORPUS
  Plain-text copies of the Wikipedia articles this set cites sit in the scratchpad folder
  named in your brief (wiki/*.txt, each with URL and revision). Read those before writing;
  reach for WebFetch when a fact is not there. A fact you could not find gets written as
  *Tradition holds —* or left out. Never invent a date, a quote, a price, a menu item or
  a person's death.
